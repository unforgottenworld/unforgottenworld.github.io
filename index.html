UNFORGOTTEN

Our Mission

Improving the lives of children with disabilities and special needs living in rural areas of low-income countries.

Our Vision

A world in which no child is deprived of access to education because of their abilities, their origin or their community. We work to remove barriers and create opportunities for every child to learn, grow, and develop.

Our Goal

Facilitate access to education, foster social inclusion and raise awareness of the challenges faced by these children.

Our Story

We are Álvaro Sampedro and María Castrillo, founders of Unforgotten. We met on a plane traveling from Madrid to Bristol, where we both lived at the time. That trip marked the beginning of our friendship, which has since been strengthened through shared experiences, including a trip to Rwanda, where the idea for Unforgotten was born. Although we are both from Madrid, our different profiles complement each other and bring a diversity of skills to the organization.

Our evolution

We started developing Unforgotten in January 2024. During this time, we have had numerous discussions with the management and staff of the Nyabihu School for the Deaf, collaborated with other charities in the region, and sought guidance from experts in the field. Unforgotten was officially registered with the UK Charity Commission in August 2025.

Location and Registration

Based in the United Kingdom, registered with the Charity Commission of England and Wales, it operates through direct collaborations in Rwanda.

Projects

Currently, all our projects are focused on our partner center: Nyabihu School for the Deaf (school website). This is the center where Álvaro volunteered for four months (November 2023 – February 2024).

Summer Camp 2025

The Unforgotten 2025 Summer Camp took place in August at the Nyabihu School for the Deaf, located in Nyabihu district, in Rwanda's Western Province. This provided a space where students could learn and develop outside the pressure of the academic year.

Main objectives

  • Improve reading comprehension (a key academic challenge)
  • Reinforce practical math skills for daily living
  • Train teachers in the use of digital tools (computers)
  • Develop vocational skills (e.g., sewing workshops)
  • Encourage artistic expression through art and dance

Impact and learnings

Summer Camp was an important milestone for Unforgotten.

  • Offered meaningful learning experiences
  • Reinforced confidence with the center
  • Laid the foundation for long-term collaboration

Impact measurement

To measure long-term impact, we have requested annual reports from the center that include:

  • Number of students enrolled
  • National exam results
  • Status of former students (education, employment, unemployment)

Key findings

  • Only 14 out of 54 students (25.9%) passed the national exams in the last five years.
  • 64% of alumni (60 out of 93) are currently unemployed or out of school.

These figures highlight the structural barriers that affect access to education and employment for deaf students.

Books for School (February 2026)

Making learning easier

In February 2026, we provided 190 textbooks to the center. Previously, most classes had only one book per subject, which was usually used by the teacher. This made it difficult for students to actively participate in classes.

Benefits of access to books

  • Students can follow classes more effectively
  • Visual learning is enhanced by images and explanations
  • Increase confidence and participation in the classroom

Now, teachers can teach in a more structured and effective way.

Opening up opportunities

This initiative helps students prepare for the National Exams, which are necessary to access secondary education.

Access to appropriate educational materials increases their chances of success and opens the door to further training and new opportunities.

"Sometimes, something as simple as a book can transform a student's future."
